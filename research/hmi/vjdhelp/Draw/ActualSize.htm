7.4.10 Actual Size of Graphical Objects

The actual size of an object is the area of its selection box—not the display area of the object itself.

  • The display area of the Dot is its actual size, which is absolute, as the Dot has no handles for resizing.

  • The display area of a rectangular object—Rectangle, Vertical Scale, Horizontal Scale, Text Object, Image Object—is the same as that of its selection box.

  • The display area of an elliptical object—Ellipse, Arc, Pie, Symmetric Polygon, Circular Scale—or a segmented line object—Line, Polyline, Polygon, Bezier Curve—is less than that of its selection box.
    When working with these objects, please note the difference between their display area and their actual size.

Object

Actual Size

Dot

A single point. The object's size is its diameter, in pixels.

Four diagonal corner lines indicate only that the dot is selected. These corner points are not handles, as the dot has no selection box.

Line

A straight line connecting two nodes—the start point and end point indicating that the line is selected—is the object's actual size. The line has no selection box, but its start and end points act as handles.

Rectangle

The object's actual size is the area of its rectangular selection box, as defined by its height and width properties. The area of the displayed object's perimeter is the same as that of its selection box.

Ellipse

The object's actual size is the area of its rectangular selection box, as defined by its height and width properties. The area of the displayed object's circumference is less than that of its selection box.

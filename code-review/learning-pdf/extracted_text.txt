1 · The plant and the power balance
A site consumes electricity, generates solar power, charges vehicles, and has a battery. The controller chooses
battery charging/discharging and sometimes reduces solar output. It tries to reduce the annual bill while
delivering EV energy and avoiding a grid trip.
Symbol / acronym
Meaning and units
FNE / PV
Fotonaponska elektrana: solar generation, kW
FEC
The site’s fixed consumption/load, kW
BESS
Battery energy storage system: 1500 kWh nominal, ±750 kW
SoC
State of charge, %. Allowed range 10–90%
EV
Electric vehicle; four chargers, up to 22 kW each
EGC / grid
Grid connection: import up to 1000 kW, export up to 499 kW
kW versus kWh
kW is power now; kWh is energy accumulated over time
The key sign convention
grid = fixed_load - solar + sum(EV_power) - battery_power
battery_power > 0  means DISCHARGE into the site
battery_power < 0  means CHARGE from the site
grid > 0           means IMPORT
grid < 0           means EXPORT
At 400 kW for one 15-minute interval, energy = 400 × 0.25 = 100 kWh. Battery losses mean that bus energy and
stored battery energy are different.

2 · Two clocks, one correct sequence
One simulated interval represents 15 minutes, but the computer may calculate it in milliseconds. The control
result must depend on the simulated step k, not the laptop clock or how quickly the PLC task repeats.
At step k: current load, available solar and starting SoC are known
Actual energy-flow readings describe interval k−1
Log k−1 using the remembered date, tariff and fixed load
Choose commands for interval k
At k+1: interval k becomes the previous interval
There are 96 intervals per day and 35,040 per year. At k = 0, reset the controller and logs. At k = 35,040, log the
last interval and issue zero commands. Do not accidentally skip that final bookkeeping call.
A normal simulation period is at least 10 ms. Earlier validation also used a 5 ms stress setting; that is a performance check, not the
permitted normal setting.

5 · Physical limits and the EV promise
Before optimisation, Team_Main calculates what the simulator can actually accept. Near the battery’s
upper/lower SoC boundary, its feasible power can be much smaller than 750 kW.
Feasible bus-side limits
usable battery energy = (SoC − minimum SoC) / 100 × capacity
max charge = min(750, remaining room / (0.25 × charge efficiency))
max discharge = min(750, usable energy × discharge efficiency / 0.25)
EV feasible power = min(22, remaining energy / (0.25 × 0.9))
With 1500 kWh capacity and a 10–90% SoC window, the usable battery window is 1200 kWh. Charge and
discharge efficiencies are 0.95 each. EV efficiency is 0.9.

tolerate being slowed down. A guard intervention can reduce EV charging to protect the grid; practice-data runs
still delivered 100%.

6 · Strategy builds the planning question
FB_Strategy connects all the pieces. In normal operation, iStrategy = 1 selects forecasting and planning. Mode 0
is the simple baseline: charge the battery on solar surplus and discharge on deficit.
Choose horizon: at most 96 intervals
Learn current conditions and forecast future net load
Read published prices and add the correct grid fee
Calculate the day-ahead learned cap floor
Plan battery power; curtail exports at negative prices
Day-ahead prices for tomorrow become visible after 13:00 (quarter-hour 52). Before that, the horizon ends at
midnight; after that it may include tomorrow, but the configured 96-step computing limit still applies. The
year-end horizon is shortened too.
aCi = energy price + import grid fee. aCe = max(0, 0.9 × energy price). Treating negative-price exports as having
no benefit is paired with actual voluntary solar curtailment when exporting would cost money.

8 · Forecasting: adjust for today
A learned profile alone would react slowly to a cloudy day or unusually busy site. Intraday correction compares
recent actual observations to their pre-update profile values and scales the forecast accordingly.
Forecast calculation
load correction = clamp(0.5, recent actual / recent profile, 1.5)
solar correction = clamp(0.2, recent actual / recent profile, 1.5)
fade = exp(−future_step / 16)
future = profile × (1 + (correction − 1) × fade)
expected net load = fixed load + predicted EV power − solar
pessimistic net = expected + ksig × deviation + (1 − pvf) × solar
Setting
S1: two tariff
S2: day ahead
ksig (load reserve)
0.75
0
pvf (solar share trusted)
0.6
0.2
Peak penalty uses
Pessimistic net load
Expected net load
At future step zero, the forecast uses current actual load and solar, so expected and pessimistic net load are
equal. Connected EVs are forecast as charging immediately; future arrivals are not magically known.
A forecast error is expected. That is why the controller replans every interval and why a separate grid guard
checks the actual current interval.

9 · Find a plausible monthly peak cap
A large monthly import spike creates a peak charge. If the month has already reached 600 kW, lowering today’s
import to 500 kW cannot undo that paid peak. The planner therefore starts with the month’s peak so far as its
cap floor.
F_PeakCap in pseudocode
test(cap):
  energy = current usable battery energy
  for each predicted interval:
    if net_load > cap:
      discharge enough to hold cap
      fail if power is too high or energy becomes negative
    else:
      recharge below cap, respecting power and capacity
  succeed
if test(already_paid_peak): return already_paid_peak
otherwise bisect between that peak and the import limit
return feasible cap × 1.02, clamped to the import limit
Bisection repeatedly halves a search range. The code tests the paid peak first, then performs up to 12
refinements. This is a short feasibility simulation, not the full dynamic program.

10 · Dynamic programming: the map
The planner compares battery choices across a horizon, rather than choosing only what looks cheapest this
minute. Its dynamic program stores the best future cost for each possible battery energy level.
State: usable stored energy, 31 levels from 0 to 1200 kWh
Action: change energy by −4 … +4 levels (40 kWh each)
Transition: energy_next = energy_now + action × 40
Cost: grid energy + soft penalty for import above target
Work backwards; then choose only the first real action
A positive DP action increases stored energy and means charging; the final output rPb uses the opposite
convention: positive means bus-side discharge. Efficiencies convert between stored-energy changes and bus
power.
Model predictive control: calculate a whole future plan, apply just its first action, then recalculate using the next
measurements. The future plan is guidance, not a fixed timetable.

11 · Dynamic programming: the arithmetic
Backward recurrence
V[last horizon, energy] = −energy × terminal energy value
for time from horizon−1 backwards to 1:
  for each allowed action:
    calculate bus power and stage cost
  for each of 31 energy states:
    Vnow[state] = minimum over feasible actions of:
      stage_cost[action] + Vnext[state + action]
  Vnext = Vnow

12 · The first action is more precise
The future DP uses a coarse 40 kWh energy grid for speed. The action taken now is evaluated more precisely
against current SoC and exact current load/solar.
First action in pseudocode
candidates = 41 powers from max_charge to max_discharge
also try:
  battery power that makes grid power exactly zero
  battery power that makes import exactly equal to cap
for each feasible candidate:
  calculate resulting stored energy
  interpolate the future DP cost between neighbouring levels
  add exact first-interval stage cost
choose the cheapest candidate
if import can be kept at cap: enforce that final correction

13 · What v7 adds: a learned cap floor
V7 avoids repeatedly choosing an unrealistically low S2 import target early in a month. A slightly higher target
can make cheap charging possible and reduce costly later peaks. This is deliberate trade-off, not a relaxation of
the 1000 kW grid limit.
FB_CapFloor in pseudocode
R_day = lowest cap that could have served a measured day
        starting with a full battery
floor = min(import_limit,
            0.95 × mean(last up to 3 completed monthly peaks),
            1.20 × max(last up to 14 daily R_day values))
activate only in S2, with at least one completed month
and at least five daily R_day samples
planner target = max(original target, floor)
The code records current fixed load minus available solar, then adds the previous interval’s actual EV draw when
that measurement becomes available. It closes the day before starting the new day’s record.
F_DayReq runs its 14-step bisection when quarter-hour ≥ 14 on the next day (03:30). The verified ST runs that
calculation in one call; the delay does not mean one bisection iteration is spread across each of 14 PLC calls.

14 · GridGuard is the last safety net
Optimisation is allowed to be approximate. Current-interval grid protection cannot wait for last interval’s grid
meter: it predicts the bus balance from current load, solar, EV requests and feasible battery power.
GridGuard in pseudocode
clamp all requests to physically feasible values
predicted_grid = fixed_load − solar + EV_sum − battery
if predicted import is too high:
  restore voluntarily curtailed solar
  increase battery discharge / reduce charging
  reduce EV power, largest slack first
  flag failure if still above the margin target
if predicted export is too high:
  reduce discharge / increase charging
  curtail solar if needed
recalculate predicted grid power from final values
Configured margins are 1 kW: the target import limit is 999 kW and export target is 498 kW, below the hard
1000/499 kW trip limits. The margin is useful headroom, but the code can report that it cannot hold the margin
when the underlying physical situation makes it impossible.
